The 2-minute version

For ten years I was involved in raising over 250 million euros for charities.

But that is not where it started. From 2005 to 2011 I played for the Dutch national rugby team. I scraped through my studies, and at the end of every month my account was empty. I had plenty of talent. But no plan.

I travelled from Vancouver to Buenos Aires, and then went into fundraising. First for the KiKa foundation, then with my own consultancy, PeerWorks, which I sold after four years. After that I worked my way up to the management team of KWF, the Dutch cancer society. From the outside everything added up: an apartment in Amsterdam, a good salary, top-flight rugby. On the inside I was empty. In 2021 I burned out, and I stopped.

In the years that followed I started again. I completed an MBA and a Young Executive Programme, ventured into a start-up, played at the 2024 Touch Rugby World Cup and developed courses about crowdfunding.

In 2025 I moved back to Zeeland to care for my grandmother. During that time I stepped onto a rugby pitch one last time. After five minutes I took a knee to the temple, and I lost months. I had to learn to work again: less on willpower, more on rest and rhythm. Then the question I had pushed away for years came back: what are you doing it for? With that brain injury, I retrained as a financial life planner.

Today I develop courses at Ooit, work for Het Zeeuwse Landschap, a nature conservation charity, and help people plan their lives. Internationals have a special place in that. I was in a relationship with an expat, and saw up close what it takes to build a life in a country that is not your own. I live in the polder where I was born. I don’t earn more than I used to. I do live better.

I work with four questions: what do you live for, what if things go wrong, what do you do every month, and what is enough? I have started over often enough to know that it can be done.

Arko

Outside work

Rugby is still a big part of my life, these days as head coach at Tovaal in Goes. When I am not on a pitch, you will find me on an old moped.
